What's changing

The register isn't a one-off form. It's a live record.

The Register your rental property service is the national database created by the Renters' Rights Act 2025. If you rent out a property in England on an assured or regulated tenancy, you must register yourself, register each property and pay an annual fee — including companies, charities, trusts and joint landlords.

You then have 28 days to supply anything outstanding, or your registration may be deactivated. After that, any change — new rent, different occupants, a renewed certificate — must be updated within 28 days.

From the day your region opens, tenants can also apply for Rent Repayment Orders where a landlord knowingly or recklessly gives false or misleading information, or keeps breaching the rules.

Most landlords' certificates are scattered across inboxes and folders. That's the real problem — and it's the one we solve.

What the register asks for
  • ✓Property details — address, bedrooms, rental status
  • ✓A correspondence address in England or Wales
  • ✓Managing agent, superior landlord and freeholder contacts
  • ✓Gas safety certificate (if there's a gas supply)
  • ✓EICR or EIC electrical certificate
  • ✓Energy Performance Certificate
  • ✓Tenancy information, including rent
  • ✓Any HMO, additional or selective licence
Source: GOV.UK, Register your rental property service: guidance for landlords (29 Oct 2026)

Regional rollout

When does your region open?

Registration is rolling out region by region. You can register early, but every let property must be registered before its region's deadline.

RegionOpensDeadline (before midnight)Status

Properties in more than one region? You can register them all at once, as long as none is registered after its region's deadline. All your properties then share one renewal date — which we track for you.
